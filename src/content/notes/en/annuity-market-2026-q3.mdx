On November 6, LIMRA — the research organization for the U.S. life insurance and annuity industry — released its third-quarter 2026 annuity sales figures. The short version: **another record**. Look inside, though, and the product types moved in quite different directions.

## $126.3 billion in one quarter, the 12th straight quarter over $100 billion

U.S. annuity sales reached **$126.3 billion** in the third quarter, up **4%** from a year earlier. That beat the record set just one quarter before, and it was the **12th consecutive quarter** above $100 billion. January through September totaled $355.0 billion, 2% more than last year.

LIMRA pointed to two reasons: **higher crediting rates after the Federal Reserve's September rate increase**, and **strong stock markets**. People wanted both to protect principal and to follow the market up.

| Type | Q3 sales | vs. Q3 last year |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-rate deferred | $44.5 billion | +2% |
| Fixed indexed | $30.7 billion | −8% |
| RILA (registered index-linked) | $25.3 billion | +22% |
| Traditional variable | $18.2 billion | +11% |
| Single premium immediate (SPIA) | $4.1 billion | +6% |

## Fixed-rate annuities follow rates right away

Fixed-rate deferred sales were $44.5 billion, up 7% from the second quarter. September alone rose more than 7% over August, which LIMRA tied to **crediting rates inching up** after the rate increase.

As I explained in [declared rate or indexed](/en/declared-vs-indexed/), a fixed-rate annuity is the simple one: it promises a set rate. So when rates rise, that change shows up there first and most clearly.

## Indexed annuities are holding back for now

Fixed indexed sales were $30.7 billion — level with the second quarter and 8% lower than a year earlier. The nine-month total is down 6%. LIMRA said the rate increase **has not yet lifted indexed sales**, which face competition from fixed-rate products that respond faster to rates and from RILAs that capture more market upside.

This doesn't mean indexed annuities got worse. The design is the same: principal protected, with a share of index gains. The numbers simply show that when rates rise, fixed-rate products tend to look better first.

## Immediate annuities grew quietly

Single premium immediate annuities — a lump sum in, monthly income starting right away — were $4.1 billion in the quarter, up 6% from last year, and up 14% for the first nine months. It reads as a sign that more people near retirement want a dependable monthly check in place.

<Callout tag="Before you compare numbers">

Starting with its January 2026 report, LIMRA changed how it counts sales. It now includes **money added to existing contracts and exchanges within the same company**, not just new contracts. That makes side-by-side comparisons with earlier years tricky. These figures are also **preliminary**.

</Callout>

## Indexed annuities: what to watch

Quarter by quarter this year, indexed sales rose 14% from the first quarter to $30.6 billion in the second, then held about level at $30.7 billion in the third. In its second-quarter release, LIMRA said cap rates were slightly below a year earlier, but **rising caps during the quarter helped lift sales**.

In January, LIMRA projected that total 2026 annuity sales would stay above $450 billion and that index-linked products — indexed annuities and RILAs — would keep growing. It pointed to "Peak65," with more than 4 million Americans turning 65 each year, and to maturing contracts putting money in motion. That forecast, however, assumed gradual rate cuts; in reality, rates went up in September.

From here on, this is my own reading. If higher rates take time to show up in caps, indexed annuities have room to pick up again. The first signal to watch is whether indexed sales rise from the prior quarter in the fourth-quarter figures. When they come out, I'll walk through them here.

## So what should I look at?

Where the whole market went is interesting. But **a product does not become right for you because many people bought it.** The order is the one from [where to start with retirement](/en/retirement-where-to-start/): set aside an emergency fund, write down your monthly gap, and only then look at products.

One practical point: if the guarantee period on your fixed-rate annuity is ending soon, check the **renewal rate** your insurer offers now that rates have moved. If you are thinking about moving to another contract, look at surrender charges and taxes first.

For the record, RILAs and variable annuities in the table are registered securities, and I don't handle them. This article is general information about market trends, not a recommendation of any product.
